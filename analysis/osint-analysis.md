# OSINT Analysis

## Purpose
Demonstrate a lawful, scoped approach to open-source intelligence in support of an internal security investigation. All subjects and artifacts are fictional.

## Intelligence Requirement
Determine whether publicly available information provides context relevant to the suspected data-loss event without treating public activity as proof of wrongdoing.

## Simulated Sources Reviewed
- Public professional profile showing an announced employment transition.
- Public conference biography describing technical specialization.
- Public company information concerning a fictional competitor.
- Public posts discussing general career changes.

## Analytical Result
The fictional public information provides **context, not proof**. A planned employment transition may increase the relevance of certain data-access behavior, but it cannot independently establish motive, intent, or unauthorized disclosure.

## Source Evaluation
| Factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Relevance | Moderate |
| Reliability | Varies by source |
| Corroboration | Required |
| Attribution confidence | Limited |
| Investigative use | Contextual lead only |

## Guardrails
- Collect only information relevant to an approved investigative purpose.
- Follow applicable law, policy, terms, and organizational authorization.
- Distinguish verified facts from inference.
- Avoid collecting unrelated sensitive personal information.
- Preserve source URL, access date, and relevant context when evidence is formally retained.
- Do not use protected characteristics as investigative risk indicators.

## Conclusion
OSINT should complement—not replace—technical evidence, interviews, policy analysis, and authorized internal records.
