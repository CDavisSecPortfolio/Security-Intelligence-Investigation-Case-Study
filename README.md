# Security Intelligence Investigation Case Study

**Independent Cybersecurity Portfolio Project | Cornel Davis | 2026**

> **Simulation Notice:** This is a fictional security-intelligence exercise created for professional development. All organizations, identities, logs, evidence, and events are simulated. It contains no real employer, law-enforcement, Tesla, or personal investigative data.

## Executive Summary
This case study demonstrates a structured investigation of a potential **insider threat and data-loss event** at fictional technology company **NovaDrive Technologies**. A privileged employee displays anomalous behavior including high-volume access to confidential engineering files, after-hours activity, removable-media use, and a transfer to an unsanctioned personal cloud service shortly before a planned departure.

The investigation integrates **human-focused investigative reasoning, simulated technical telemetry, OSINT research, evidence preservation, risk analysis, and cross-functional escalation**. The objective is to establish facts, preserve reliable evidence, assess business impact, and provide leadership with defensible recommendations without assuming guilt before the evidence supports a conclusion.

## Scenario
NovaDrive Technologies develops proprietary autonomous-systems components. Monitoring generates alerts involving **Employee E-1047**, an engineer with authorized access to confidential design repositories. The account shows activity outside the employee's normal baseline, bulk access to restricted files, removable-media interaction, and an upload to a non-corporate cloud destination.

No single alert proves malicious intent. The investigation tests alternative explanations, correlates independent evidence sources, documents limitations, and uses proportional escalation.

## Investigative Questions
1. Was the access authorized and consistent with a legitimate business purpose?
2. What confidential information was accessed, staged, copied, or transferred?
3. Which user, endpoint, account, device, and destination were involved?
4. Do technical and human-source facts corroborate each other?
5. Is there evidence of data loss, policy violation, technology misuse, or attempted IP theft?
6. What evidence must be preserved?
7. Which actions should be coordinated with Legal, HR, Security, and technical forensics?
8. What immediate and long-term controls should leadership consider?

## Key Finding
The simulated evidence supports a **high-confidence finding of unauthorized data transfer and policy violation**, while the available evidence alone is insufficient to establish criminal intent or prove that a competitor received the information. This distinction demonstrates evidence-based investigative judgment.

## Recommended Response
Preserve relevant evidence; coordinate with Legal and HR; validate business justification; restrict unnecessary access through approved procedures; conduct forensic review of the endpoint and transfer destination; determine the sensitivity and scope of affected data; document decisions; and address control gaps involving least privilege, removable media, cloud-app governance, and anomaly detection.
